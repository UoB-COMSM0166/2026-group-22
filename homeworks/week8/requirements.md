# Research Report on User Experience and Task Load of the Game System

## Part 1: Easy Mode

This study evaluates the user experience quality of the game system. Using a quantitative survey method, 16 participants were assessed on **System Usability** (based on SUS dimensions) and **Task Load** (based on NASA-TLX dimensions). 

### Summary of Findings
1. The game system possesses exceptionally high usability and a low barrier to entry.
2. Users demonstrate high operational confidence.
3. While the system is easy to navigate, the inherent nature of the tasks requires a significant level of mental effort and cognitive engagement.

---

### 1. Analysis of System Usability (SUS dimensions)
Based on the distribution of user responses, the system demonstrates superior performance in terms of accessibility and consistency:

**① Ease of Use and Complexity** Every participant agreed that the system is easy to use, with half of the group expressing strong agreement. Conversely, there was a unanimous consensus that the system is not unnecessarily complex. This inverse correlation confirms that the interface design is streamlined and intuitive.

**② Learning Curve and Technical Support** The vast majority of users felt they could operate the system independently. Data shows that nearly 90% of participants did not require technical assistance. Furthermore, most users disagreed with the notion that extensive prior learning was necessary, suggesting that the system's “walk-up-and-use” factor is very high.

**③ System Integration and Consistency** Participants perceived the various functions of the system to be well-integrated. The feedback indicates a high level of design harmony, with very few users identifying any inconsistencies in how the system operates.

**④ User Confidence** Most respondents reported feeling very confident while using the system. There were no reports of hesitation or fear of making errors, which indicates that the feedback loops and navigation paths are clear and reassuring to the user.

---

### 2. Quantitative Analysis of Task Load (NASA-TLX)
This section evaluates the physical and mental demands placed on the user during task execution (Scale 0-20):

**① Mental Demand** The average score was **7.13**. While generally low, 25% of users rated this above 15, indicating that certain segments of the task require high levels of concentration.

**② Effort and Performance** “Effort” was the highest-rated load factor with a mean of **8.13**. However, the “Self-Performance” score remained high at **13.63**, suggesting that while users had to work hard, they felt successful in achieving their goals.

**③ Frustration and Pressure** The scores for “Time Pressure” (**6.38**) and “Frustration” (**4.88**) were significantly low. This confirms that the system does not cause emotional distress or overwhelming urgency during the work process.

---

## Part 2: Hard Mode

This section evaluates a high-difficulty variant of the game system. 16 participants were tested under conditions of increased mechanical complexity and stricter time constraints.

### 1. Analysis of System Usability (SUS dimensions)
**① Ease of Use and Complexity** Only 18% of participants agreed the system was easy to use. 75% of users felt the system was “unnecessarily complex”.

**② Learning Curve and Technical Support** The “walk-up-and-use” factor was non-existent. Over 80% of participants reported they would need to consult a manual or tutorial.

**③ System Integration and Consistency** 45% of users perceived a “clash” between mechanics, suggesting design harmony breaks down under pressure.

**④ User Confidence** Confidence scores plummeted. 60% of respondents reported feeling “hesitant” or “anxious” during play.

### 2. Quantitative Analysis of Task Load (NASA-TLX)
**① Mental Demand** The average score surged to **17.25**. 90% of users rated this above 15.

**② Effort and Performance** “Effort” was extremely high at **18.12**, while “Self-Performance” dropped to **5.44**.

**③ Frustration and Pressure** “Time Pressure” (**15.80**) and “Frustration” (**16.15**) became the dominant factors.

---

## Part 3: Comparative Discussion and Conclusion

### 1. Core Findings and Comparison
**① Alignment with User Habits** In Easy Mode, high consistency correlated with a 75% retention intent. In Hard Mode, this intent dropped to 20%.

**② Conversion of Simplicity to Confidence** The data proves a direct causal link: low complexity leads to 100% confidence, while high complexity leads to a 60% confidence deficit.

**③ Task Challenge vs. Tool Support** In Easy Mode, users felt the tooling was smooth (Effort 8.13 vs. Frustration 4.88). In Hard Mode, tools felt inadequate (Frustration 16.15 vs. Effort 18.12).

### 2. Final Conclusion
The original system (Easy Mode) is an ideal baseline for mass-market usability. However, the Hard Mode simulation reveals that the system's design harmony is fragile under high cognitive load. To maintain performance while increasing difficulty, future iterations must provide better “Tool Support”.
